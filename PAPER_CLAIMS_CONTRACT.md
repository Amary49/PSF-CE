# Paper Claims Contract

## Allowed

- PSF-CE shows the highest descriptive mean NMI on the revised Core-10 cohort.
- PSF-CE is third by descriptive mean ACC on the revised Core-10 cohort.
- The revised Core-10 NMI comparisons do not reach Holm-corrected significance.
- The NMI-oriented profile is associated with reduced true-class fragmentation and localized residual mixing at the partition level.
- MI and AMI have favorable exploratory directions for 5/6 and 4/6 comparators, respectively.
- The native-output and no-Chameleon sensitivity analyses give the same top-level exploratory classifications.
- The post-freeze one-factor sensitivity sweep shows bounded variation on the prespecified revised-Core-10 grids and was not used for parameter reselection.

## Must be qualified

- Use “exploratory structural evidence” rather than “mechanism proof”.
- Use “post-freeze revised formal cohort” rather than “independent holdout”.
- Describe Table II as exploratory directional profiles, not significant-test counts or causal mediation.
- State that MCLA and HBGF are controlled modern PyMetis reimplementations.
- State the YACHT compatibility patch, RANGE's globally frozen local setting, and GPEC's frozen-pool wrapper/source-lock limitations.
- State that raw feature data, prediction archives, the other 24 frozen-BP pools, and third-party author repositories are not redistributed; the six exact Iris/Mushroom pools are included under CC BY 4.0 with attribution.

## Forbidden

- “PSF-CE significantly outperforms all baselines.”
- “PSF-CE preserves more information” without a narrow metric definition and statistical qualification.
- “Completeness is the sole cause of the NMI gain.”
- “Residual errors are concentrated in the largest clusters.”
- “The upstream spectral component causally explains the gain.”
- “All methods returned exactly the target number of clusters.”
- “Core-10 is completely independent of development.”
- “The frozen operating point is optimal on revised Core-10.”
- “Positive NMI differences are MI-dominant overall.”
